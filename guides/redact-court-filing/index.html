How to redact a court filing (FRCP 5.2 and Bankruptcy Rule 9037)

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 5.2(a) and Bankruptcy Rule 9037(a) let a filing include only the last four digits of a Social Security or taxpayer-ID number and of a financial-account number, only the year of a birth date, and only a minor’s initials. PDF Redact’s court-filing format applies the number and date rules automatically: it leaves the last four digits and the birth year visible and removes the rest.

Many state courts have similar rules, for example California Rules of Court, rule 1.201. Check your court’s local rules; some require more.

Steps

  1. Open the filing in PDF Redact. It recognises legal documents and applies the Legal / Court preset.
  2. Open the detection settings (Filters, or Auto-redact in newer versions) and turn on Court filing format.
  3. Names stay fully covered. For a minor, uncover their initials by hand.
  4. Export. The exported PDF keeps all unredacted text searchable, which many e-filing systems require for briefs, and the audit confirms nothing redacted remains.

Avoid the classic mistake

Several high-profile filings have been exposed because black boxes were drawn over text that could still be copied, including a 2019 filing by Paul Manafort’s lawyers. Exported pages in PDF Redact are rebuilt from what is visible, and the audit checks that no redacted text remains underneath.
