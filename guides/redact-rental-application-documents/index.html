What to redact in a rental application

A landlord needs to see that you are who you say you are and that you earn enough; they rarely need your full account numbers, every purchase or your ID number. For each document, keep your name, the dates and the income or balance figures, and hide account numbers, transactions you don’t need to show, and ID numbers. Ask before redacting if the landlord uses a screening service.

Before you send anything

  • Confirm the listing and the landlord or agent are real before sending financial documents.
  • If they use a tenant-screening service for credit and background checks, your SSN goes to that service through its own secure form, not by email.
  • Never change numbers on a document; only remove information.

Questions

Will a landlord reject redacted documents?
Most accept them if names, dates and amounts are readable. Some require originals; ask first.
